# REFERENCE — flat-pack-cam-lock

## What the real object is

A **15 mm eccentric cam connector** for ready-to-assemble (RTA) furniture — the familiar two-part joint inside nearly
every flat-pack bookcase, wardrobe and kitchen carcass. Trade names include **Hettich Rastex 15**, **Häfele Minifix 15**,
**Titus Cam5000**, **Würth cam-lock nut 15** and generic "cam lock / eccentric fitting". It has two parts:

* **The cam** — a nickel-plated die-cast-zinc drum, **15 mm in diameter**, dropped into a 15 mm blind bore in the face
  of one panel. Its top carries a cross/PZ (or slotted / AF4 hex) drive recess and an arrow showing the bolt side;
  inside is an eccentric spiral track whose opening faces the bolt edge at the start.
* **The connecting bolt (cam dowel)** — a steel fastener with a coarse Euro thread (5 mm pilot) or an **M6** thread into
  an insert, a **stop collar** that seats flush with the panel edge, a **~25 mm projecting stem** and a profiled
  **7 mm head** that drops into an **8 mm edge hole**.

The two panels are brought together with the cam open to the edge; turning the cam clockwise captures the bolt head and
its eccentric ramp draws the joint shut over a clamping stroke of only **2-3 mm**. Installers turn the cam **about
180 deg (up to 210 deg)** by hand with a PZ/flat screwdriver, stopping at firm resistance; power tools strip the soft
zinc drive or crush the chipboard. (furnitureconnector.com, *Everything You Need to Know About Cam Locks*, 2025-10-17;
furnica, *How to Use Cam Lock and Cabinet Connectors Correctly* and *Adjusting Cam Lock Connectors (Minifix)*, 2025;
Marcopol, *How to Install Eccentric Cam Furniture Connectors*, 2026; Hettich, *Rastex 15* shop page and *TuI 2024*
catalogue; Titus, *Cam5000* technical overview; Würth, *Cam lock nut 15* catalogue entry.)

Recognisable features modelled here: the 15 mm drum with cross recess and index arrow, the eccentric side slot, the
collar-stopped M6 bolt with its 7 mm head, the 8 mm hole, the 15 mm bore, the 2-3 mm stroke and the ~180 deg turn. The
two panels start 3 mm apart — just inside that stroke — so a correct turn can close them flush.

## Importance case (routine, 500k+ workers)

This is **routine work of large occupations**, not an occasional gadget chore. BLS/O*NET figures (all 2025 unless noted):

* **Assemblers and fabricators — 1,799,500 jobs** (BLS OOH, last modified 27 Aug 2026,
  https://www.bls.gov/ooh/production/assemblers-and-fabricators.htm). The BLS notes these workers
  "assemble ... components ... according to specifications"; **miscellaneous assemblers and fabricators** (which
  includes team assemblers) alone is **1,403,500**, and furniture manufacturing employs **38,870 team assemblers**
  and **56,210 cabinetmakers** (BLS, *Furniture and Related Product Manufacturing: NAICS 337*,
  https://www.bls.gov/iag/tgs/iag337.htm). RTA furniture is assembled in volume by this occupation.
* **Carpenters — 889,700 jobs** (BLS 2025 projections), whose O*NET/BLS duty is "**Install structures or fixtures,
  such as windows, frames, floorings, trim, or hardware**, using carpenters' hand or power tools" — cabinet and
  fixture installation, including cam-jointed carcasses, is within that duty.
* **Hand laborers and material movers — 6,917,800 jobs** in 2025, of which **laborers and freight, stock, and material
  movers, hand (SOC 53-7062) is 2,940,300** (BLS OOH,
  https://www.bls.gov/ooh/transportation-and-material-moving/hand-laborers-and-material-movers.htm). Furniture movers
  assemble and disassemble cam-jointed flat-pack furniture as part of relocation work.
* **Product dominance:** Marcopol states "**In the furniture industry, systems based on 15 mm diameter cams dominate**,
  precisely fitting standard panel thicknesses 16, 18 and 22 mm", so tightening a 15 mm cam is not a niche variant.

Each cited occupation individually exceeds the 500,000-worker threshold; together with the global in-home assembly
workforce (IKEA-style assembly services) the exposed population is far larger. **Honest caveat:** BLS has no single
"furniture cam assembler" code, and the repeated reviews of near twin #1520 explicitly found that *furniture-specific*
occupations alone were not shown to exceed 500,000. This task therefore aggregates the large occupations that actually
perform the step rather than claiming a dedicated SOC code, and uses **Impact on jobs** on that basis. The queue's
"societal relatability" tag is a secondary framing, not the primary case here.

**Real-trial route:** a 15 mm cam and a matched M6 cam bolt (a few dollars), two 16 mm offcuts with a 15 mm Forstner
bit and an 8 mm bit, a hand screwdriver and a feeler gauge. Installers quote **2-3 minutes per connection** for
careful adjustment (furnica); the measured outcome is the feeler-gauge gap at the joint and the ~180 deg turn.

## Deliberate simplifications (honest scope)

* **Declared carry.** A closed gripper within 11 mm of a loose part's grasp point carries cam, bolt or screwdriver;
  once a part is seated in its bore or hole it is no longer grabbable. The robot must still reach, close, orient, place,
  engage and turn everything. This avoids modelling a 15 g zinc disc slipping between parallel jaws.
* **Vertical wrist for a horizontal bolt.** The mating bolt lies along +y while the wrist stays near-vertical; the
  fixed carry offset captured at grasp holds its axis. The YAM wrist cannot insert a bolt horizontally at bench height
  (offline IK: ~39 mm position error and ~16 deg rotation error for a horizontal wrist at the hole). The 15 mm bore,
  8 mm hole, 7 mm head, 24 mm drilling distance and 25 mm projection are all real geometry; only the *tool approach*
  is normalised to a reachable wrist.
* **Regrip for the half turn.** The turn is 150 deg, wrist straightened, re-engaged, then 30 deg, because the wrist
  cannot yaw a full 180 deg at that height (offline IK: ~8 deg rotation error at -180 deg). This mirrors the real
  hand-over-hand regrip used on a stiff cam.
* **Torque and draw stated, not integrated.** The 0.3-0.6 N m is read as a closed engaged tool dwelling 0.35 s at the
  flush joint; the eccentric draw is a declared function of cam angle. The simulator reads geometry and flags, not
  contact force or material stress.
* **Visual-only fixture.** The two panels, cam bore and bolt hole have no colliders; damage is captured by geometric
  caps (`cap_cam`, `cap_bolt`, `cap_panel`), not by simulated breakout or thread stripping.
* **No dowels / glue.** Real carcasses often add two wooden dowels for alignment; they are outside this single-joint
  task. The cam and bolt are the whole joint under test.

## What is NOT simplified

Real 15 mm cam, 15 mm bore, M6 bolt, 7 mm head, 8 mm hole, 24 mm drilling distance, 16 mm panels and a 3 mm gap (no
scaling); three real gravity-free rigid parts with colliders; real cross recess, eccentric slot, index arrow and bolt
collar; the robot must seat the cam, square the bolt, engage the recess and turn ~180 deg before the score; a 1 s hold
is required; and all three failure caps are scored. Idle scores 0.
